## 3. Enroll in the Apple Developer Program

The current team `B8SM9PP3KC` is a free **Personal Team**: its provisioning profiles expire after 7 days, and it cannot distribute on TestFlight or the App Store.

1. On an iPhone, iPad or Mac signed in with the Apple Account you want to use (for example the one already signed in to Xcode), make sure **two-factor authentication** is on.
2. Easiest route: install the **Apple Developer** app from the App Store, open it, choose **Account › Enroll Now**, and follow the prompts. Alternatively go to <https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/> in a browser.
3. Choose the entity type:
   * **Individual / sole proprietor:** fastest. Your personal legal name is shown as the seller on the App Store.
   * **Organization:** requires a legal entity and a **D-U-N-S number** (free, can take days to weeks). The company name is shown as the seller.
4. Enter your legal name and address exactly as on your ID, and pay the annual fee (99 USD, or the local price in SEK).
5. Wait for the confirmation email. It usually arrives within 48 hours; organizations can take longer.
6. After approval, in **Xcode › Settings › Accounts**, select your Apple Account and check that a team with your name and the role **Account Holder** is listed. The team ID may differ from `B8SM9PP3KC`; that is fine.
7. In App Store Connect (<https://appstoreconnect.apple.com>), go to **Business** (previously Agreements, Tax and Banking):
   * Accept the **Free Apps Agreement** (shown automatically).
   * For a **paid** app, also sign the **Paid Apps Agreement** and fill in banking and tax details (including the US tax form W-8BEN for individuals outside the US).
   * Declare your **EU trader status** (Digital Services Act). This is required before the app can be offered in the EU.

## 4. Configure Signing & Capabilities

1. Open `The Last Rail.xcodeproj` in Xcode.
2. Select the **The Last Rail** project in the navigator, then the **The Last Rail** target, then the **Signing & Capabilities** tab.
3. Make sure **Automatically manage signing** is checked.
4. **Team:** choose your *paid* team (your name, not "(Personal Team)").
5. **Bundle Identifier:** keep `Victor-Svensson.The-Last-Rail`, or change it now (see §2).
6. Xcode registers the App ID and creates the certificates and profiles. The line under "Signing Certificate" should show *Apple Development* without errors.
7. Repeat the Team choice for the `The Last RailTests` and `The Last RailUITests` targets, so tests can run on a device.
8. **Capabilities:** none are needed. Do not add Game Center, iCloud or push notifications; the game doesn't use them.
9. Optional check: connect your iPhone, choose it as the run destination, and press **⌘R**. The first time, enable **Developer Mode** on the iPhone (Settings › Privacy & Security › Developer Mode).

## 6. Archive and upload the game

> ⚠️ Uploading sends the build to Apple. Do this only when you are ready. It does **not** publish the app: nothing reaches the public until you submit for review **and** release.

1. In Xcode, choose **Product › Destination › Any iOS Device (arm64)**.
2. Optional: raise the build number if you have uploaded before. Target › General › **Build** (each upload needs a new build number; the version can stay 1.0).
3. Choose **Product › Archive**. When it finishes, the **Organizer** window opens with the archive "The Last Rail 1.0 (1)".
4. Optional but recommended: click **Validate App** first. This checks the build with App Store Connect without uploading it.
5. Click **Distribute App** › **App Store Connect** › **Upload**, and keep the defaults (automatic signing, upload symbols on, manage version and build number on).
6. Xcode creates the **Apple Distribution** certificate and the App Store profile automatically, then uploads.
7. After 5–30 minutes the build appears under App Store Connect › your app › **TestFlight** with status "Processing" and then "Ready to Submit". Apple sends an email when processing finishes.
8. Export compliance: because `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = NO` is set, App Store Connect does not ask the encryption question.

Command-line alternative (after enrollment and signing in to Xcode):

```bash
export DEVELOPER_DIR=/Volumes/WD-BLACK/User/Apps/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer
xcodebuild archive -project "The Last Rail.xcodeproj" -scheme "The Last Rail" -configuration Release -destination 'generic/platform=iOS' -archivePath build/TheLastRail.xcarchive -allowProvisioningUpdates
```

```bash
xcodebuild -exportArchive -archivePath build/TheLastRail.xcarchive -exportOptionsPlist ExportOptions.plist -exportPath build/export -allowProvisioningUpdates
```

The repository's `ExportOptions.plist` is set to `destination = export`, which only creates a local `.ipa`. Change it to `upload` only when you intend to send the build to Apple.

## 8. Invite testers

**Internal testers** (up to 100 people on your App Store Connect team; no review needed):
1. **Users and Access** › **＋** › add the person's email address and give them a role (for example *Developer* or *Marketing*). They accept the email invitation.
2. TestFlight › **Internal Testing** › **＋** › create a group (for example "Kärnteam") › add the users › enable the build.

**External testers** (up to 10,000; the first build needs a short **Beta App Review**):
1. TestFlight › **External Testing** › **＋** › create a group (for example "Speltestare").
2. Add testers by email, or turn on a **Public Link** (with an optional tester limit) that you can share.
3. Add the build to the group › **Submit for Review**. Approval usually takes under 24–48 hours.

Testers install **TestFlight** from the App Store and accept the invitation. Builds expire after 90 days.

## 11. Submit for App Review

1. Make sure everything on the version page is filled in (App Store Connect marks missing fields in red), and that a build is selected.
2. Click **Add for Review** (top right), then **Submit to App Review** in the dialog.
3. Status changes to *Waiting for Review*, then *In Review*. Review usually takes 24–48 hours but can take longer. Approval is never guaranteed.
4. If Apple rejects the app, the **App Review** messages explain why. Reply in Resolution Center or fix and upload a new build (with a higher build number). Prepared answers to likely questions are in `AppStore/APP_REVIEW_NOTES.md`.

## 12. Release the app after approval

Before submitting, choose under **Version Release** on the version page:
* **Manually release this version:** after approval the status is *Pending Developer Release*; click **Release This Version** when you are ready (recommended for a first launch).
* **Automatically release this version:** goes live immediately after approval.
* **Automatically release after App Review, no earlier than [date]:** for a planned launch date.

Optional: **Phased Release for Automatic Updates** only affects updates, not version 1.0.

After release the app appears in the App Store within a few hours (search can take up to 24 hours). For future versions, raise `MARKETING_VERSION` (for example 1.0.1 or 1.1) and the build number, archive, upload, and fill in "What's New".
